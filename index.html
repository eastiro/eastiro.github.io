01 About

I’m a student at the Academy of AIAT, Xi’an Jiaotong-Liverpool University, working mostly in Python and TypeScript on problems where the interesting part is not the model but the evidence: showing where a number came from and being explicit about what it does not prove.

My projects tend to start with a narrow question — could a different final pit call have changed a Grand Prix result? where does a flight delay stop propagating? — and end with a reproducible pipeline, an audit trail and a stated boundary on the conclusions. Several of them report negative results on purpose, because a tool that abstains honestly is more useful than one that guesses.

This site is hand-written HTML and CSS with no framework or build step. It ships in a few kilobytes and loads without a single external request, which is a deliberate engineering choice rather than a limitation.

CS Tactical Lens

A workbench that turns real CS2 match demos into reviewable cross-team comparisons of pre-contact positioning. Every finding links back to the exact round, radar map and equipment HUD so a reader can check it tick by tick.

An interactive study of how flight delays propagate and recover along real aircraft rotations, and how they co-occur with weather and schedule congestion. Bilingual, with three delay thresholds and a stated scientific boundary.

Motorsport research

F1 Strategy Lab

An explainable strategy study built on public OpenF1 and FastF1 data: a wet-to-slick pit-call sandbox for one Grand Prix, plus a five-season audit of whether public signals can support that decision at all. The honest answer is mostly no.
